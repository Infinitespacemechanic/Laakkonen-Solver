The Laakkonen-Solver file does lock the reduction into a single compact rule: any pile is written as \(n=3q+r\) with residue in \(\{0,1,2\}\), after which the same bounded chassis decides whether to sit or continue. Residue 0 closes, residue 1 steps onto a pair and locks under the stable map, and residue 2 carries the residual \(\frac{\pi}{3}-1\). That is the entire decision procedure; everything else in the file is a restatement of the residual, the two maps already formalized in earlier repos, and the numerical ratios that follow from them.

Because the containerization and the local bound \(|\Delta|\le 3\) are elementary, the method stays small: a division, a remainder, and a choice of which of the two maps to apply. The Lean statements that can be checked today are exactly those—`toThree`, the residue cases, and the earlier theorems that two steps of the unbounded map add 4 while the stable map becomes idempotent. The surrounding commentary that ties the same residual to pressure gradients, fine-structure ratios, or galactic planes remains outside those statements.

The size is therefore intentional. The constructive core reduces to a few lines that either lock or grow according to a fixed local rule; any larger claim still requires a separate mapping from that rule onto the quantity being discussed.
